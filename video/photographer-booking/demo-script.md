# Sara for wedding photographers — demo script

Six screens, A–F, roughly 60 seconds. Each screen carries one beat of the
four-line description below, and the words are written against what is
actually on the screen in **Version 21** of the booking app.

**The description this script delivers:**

> Sara is your studio's receptionist. She answers the phone 24/7, 365 days a
> year — couples, planners, venues, all of it — and asks what you'd have
> asked: the date, the venue, the budget.
>
> She checks your calendar on the call, so "are you free on the 14th?" gets an
> answer in seconds instead of on Monday, and holds the date while they decide.
>
> She knows whether it's a couple enquiring or another service provider wanting
> to collaborate — a makeup artist, a hair stylist, a caterer, a decorator —
> and groups them accordingly.
>
> Whenever you next look, every enquiry and every collaboration is there in one
> place — collected, sorted, with what each one is worth and who needs chasing
> today.

**Before you record:** Bookings tab → *Reset demo data* at the bottom, so dates
and follow-up counts start clean. On the Calls tab set the filter to **Today** —
that gives exactly five calls, and the stat tile reads **5 Calls answered**, so
the number you say matches the number on screen. Record on a phone, or add the
link to your home screen so there is no browser chrome.

**Strongest possible opening:** play 15 seconds of Sara actually answering. Ring
your own Retell number, ask "are you free on the 14th of December", record it,
and lay that audio under screens A and B. Everything after is a mockup — that
part is not, and they will hear the difference.

---

### A · CALLS tab — filter on Today, banner at the top, then scroll

> Sara is your studio's receptionist. She answers the phone 24/7, 365 days a year.
> Five calls today — couples, a planner, a venue, all of it. You were shooting; she picked up every one.
> And every one of them is recorded. That little play button is the call itself.

### B · Tap Priya Nair — the player, then FROM THE CALL

> This is Priya. She's getting married on the 14th of December and wants to know if you're free.
> Sara asked exactly what you'd have asked: Taj Krishna, six hundred guests, budget around two lakh.
> And if you want to hear how she said it — two minutes, forty-one seconds, right there.

### C · Back, then tap Tejas — Falaknuma Events

> And this is Tejas, from Falaknuma Palace. Not a bride — a venue, calling for the first time about working together.
> So Sara asked a completely different set of questions: who they are, how many weddings they do, what they actually want.
> Forty a year, and they want you on their panel. Same receptionist, a completely different conversation.

### D · DATES tab — scroll to "Asked today", then up to the calendar

> She checks your calendar while they're still on the phone. "Are you free on the 14th?" — free, and she held it.
> Vikram asked about the 8th. That one's booked, and he was told so, on the call.
> Black is booked, amber is a date she's holding, green is yours to sell. The 14th and 15th are amber because two couples are deciding right now.

### E · BOOKINGS tab — the pills, then tap Partners

> Eight couples open, four confirmed, and six vendors — she sorted them as they rang.
> A hair stylist, a caterer, a decorator, a planner, a venue. Not one of them is a bride, and none are mixed in with the couples.
> Nisha has sent you six weddings. Shruti and Tejas called for the first time today.

### F · BOOKINGS — back to Open

> And this is the part you'd otherwise keep in your head.
> Kavya's hold expires this evening. Vikram needs a final price tomorrow. Nikhil's on his fourth follow-up — probably time to let that one go.
> Not a list of names. A list of what to do, and when.

### G · Optional close — log Advance received → ₹60,000 → Submit, then DATES

> She's paid. One tap — advance received, sixty thousand, booking confirmed.
> The 14th turns black on its own. You never opened the calendar.
> December fills up early. If it's useful, I can have this answering your number by the weekend.

Not part of the four description lines, so cut it if you are tight for time.
It is still the best ending available: it is the only moment where you change
something and the calendar reacts without being touched.

## Notes for the recording

- **The spine of this pitch is organisation, not volume.** A working
  photographer is not short of enquiries — couples and vendors ring all the
  time. What they do not have is one place where all of it is written down,
  sorted, and ready to act on. A asks the question; E and F answer it.

- **A, B and C are the sale.** Someone answered the phone properly while you
  had a camera in your hands. Everything after is bookkeeping.

- **B and C are the same receptionist having two different conversations.** A
  couple gets asked about their date and budget; a venue gets asked what they
  do and what they want. That is not a call log sorting itself. Linger on B,
  move briskly through C — by then the audience can read the screen, so the
  only new information is *how different it looks*, and that reads in two
  seconds.

- **On the recording:** mention it in A as an aside, prove it in B by tapping
  play. Let the bar move for two seconds before you carry on talking. A
  progress bar advancing does more for credibility than any sentence about
  call recording.

- **C lands harder with Tejas than with a planner.** He is a *first contact*.
  An established partner would ring you again anyway; a venue that called once
  and got voicemail just moves down their list. The screen says
  **Relationship: First contact** without you making the point. The last line
  of C carries the third description line — say it slowly.

- **D has a second exchange worth using.** Vikram asking about the 8th and
  being told it is *booked* proves she does not just say yes — she reads the
  real calendar. A receptionist who only ever says "let me check and call
  back" is no use.

- **E matches your words exactly.** You say *makeup artist, hair stylist,
  caterer, decorator* and all four are on screen with their trade beside the
  name. Two of the six read **first contact** — Shruti and Tejas — so the
  opening and this screen close a loop.

- **F's strongest line is Nikhil.** *"Fourth follow-up — then let it go"* is
  the app telling you to stop, and that is unusual enough to be memorable.
  Most software only ever tells you to do more.

- **Understate it.** Photographers are craftspeople and will push back on being
  told they are losing money. Describe the Saturday, give the number, stop.
  Let them do the arithmetic — they will, and their figure will convince them
  more than yours.

- **Never say "AI" or "app".** She answered the phone and held the date; how
  she did it is not the sale. Likewise never close on staffing cost — the
  moment you say "cheaper than hiring someone" you are priced against a
  part-time assistant instead of a ₹2 lakh wedding.

- Tap, then speak. Keep your finger off the screen while you talk.
