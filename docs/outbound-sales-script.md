# Outbound cold-call script — selling Sara to wedding photographers

One goal per call: **a 10–15 minute demo, with a day and time confirmed.**
Nothing else counts. Not interest, not "send me details" — a slot.

The opener below is profile-led, which only works because the leads in the CRM
came from Instagram: you have their handle, their follower count, their city
and usually their work in front of you. Open the profile before you dial. A
specific sentence about their work is the whole reason the call survives the
first ten seconds; a generic one makes you the fourth marketing call that day.

---

## The spine — 25 seconds

> **Open** — "Hi, is this Rohit? … Rohit, my name is Kishore, from Nine Square. I came across your work on Instagram — the Jaipur wedding set. Completely a cold call, I'll be honest. Can I take sixty seconds?"
>
> **Reason** — "I looked at your page because I'm working with wedding photographers in Hyderabad on one specific thing — the enquiry calls that come in while you're on a shoot."
>
> **The thing** — "We put a receptionist on your number who answers 24/7. She takes the date, the venue, the guest count and the budget, checks your calendar on the call, and holds the date if you're free. When you finish shooting, every enquiry is written down and sorted."
>
> **The ask** — "I'd rather show you than describe it. Ten, fifteen minutes on a screen share — are mornings or evenings easier for you?"

## The call, in full

### 1 · Open — name, source, permission

> "Hi, is this Rohit?"
>
> *(wait for yes — always wait)*
>
> "Rohit, my name is Kishore, I'm calling from Nine Square in Hyderabad. I came
> across your work online — your Instagram, the Jaipur wedding set. This is a
> cold call, I won't pretend otherwise. Can I take sixty seconds, or have I
> caught you on a shoot?"

**Three things are doing work here.** Your name before your company. The
specific thing you saw, named. And naming it as a cold call — which costs you
nothing and buys back the trust the next sentence needs.

**If they're on a shoot:** stop immediately. "Understood — I'll not take your
time. Is tomorrow morning better, or shall I send it on WhatsApp?" Then go.
Ending the call yourself is worth more than the thirty seconds you'd win.

### 2 · Reason for the call — before any product

> "The reason I looked you up: I'm working with wedding photographers here on
> one specific thing. The enquiry calls that come in while you're holding a
> camera. Couples ring, planners ring — and most of it goes to voicemail or a
> note you meant to write down."

Do not ask "do you miss calls?" — a working photographer will say no on
reflex, and then has to defend it. State the situation and let them agree or
correct you. If they correct you — *"my wife handles the phone"* — that is the
best thing that can happen. Ask who she is and keep going; you now know who
the demo is for.

### 3 · What it is — three sentences, no more

> "What we do is put a receptionist on your number. She answers 24/7 — 365
> days — and asks what you'd ask: the date, the venue, how many guests, what
> they're spending. She checks your calendar while they're still on the phone,
> so 'are you free on the 14th of December' gets an answer in seconds instead
> of on Monday, and she holds the date while they decide.
>
> And she knows a couple from a vendor. A makeup artist or a decorator calling
> about work doesn't end up mixed in with your brides."

**Never say "AI", "bot", "software" or "app" first.** If they ask — and they
often do — answer plainly: *"Yes, it's an AI voice. You'll hear it in the
demo; I'd rather you judge it than take my word."* Honest, and it moves
straight to the ask.

### 4 · The ask — demo, with two options

> "I'd rather show you than describe it. Ten to fifteen minutes, screen share
> on WhatsApp — you'll see what she captured from a real call, and the calendar
> filling up. Are mornings easier for you, or after six?"

**Two options, never "would you be interested".** An either/or question gets
answered; a yes/no question gets "send me details".

**Then nail it down.** Day, time, and the number to call on — repeat it back:
*"So Wednesday, eleven o'clock, this same number. I'll send a WhatsApp
confirmation now."* An unconfirmed demo is not a demo.

### 5 · If a slot won't close — the downgrade, in order

Never leave the call empty-handed. Take the next thing down the list:

1. **Let them hear it instead of seeing it.** *"Then do this — call this number
   when you have a minute, and the receptionist will answer you as if you were
   a couple. Ask her if you're free on the 14th of December. Ninety seconds,
   no appointment."* This is the strongest fallback in the whole script: zero
   commitment, and the product sells itself in one call.
2. **A WhatsApp with a 60-second video**, and explicit permission to follow up:
   *"If I send it across, would you be opposed to my checking back next week?"*
3. **A better time**, from them, written into the CRM as the follow-up date.
4. **Their email**, if they insist on email — the weakest outcome, but log it.

### 6 · If you reach someone else

Studios are often answered by an assistant, a brother, or the second shooter.

> "I may have the wrong person — who looks after the enquiry calls and the
> booking diary there?"

Then: the owner's name, the best time to catch them, and whether WhatsApp on
this number reaches them. Be brief and be pleasant. Do not pitch to someone
who cannot say yes — you will spend your one good opener on the wrong person.

## Objections — short answers, then back to the ask

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "I answer my own calls." | "I'm sure you do, and that's exactly why I called — you answer them *when you're free*. The ones at eleven at night, or mid-ceremony, are the ones I'm talking about. Fifteen minutes and you can tell me whether that's a real problem for you." |
| "How much?" | "It starts around ₹X a month, and I'd rather you see it before we talk price — if it's not useful, the price doesn't matter. Mornings or evenings?" *(Never quote a figure you aren't ready to honour. If you don't have pricing settled, say: "I'll give you the exact number on the demo — I don't want to guess on a call.")* |
| "Send me details on WhatsApp." | "I'll do that now. But the thing is hard to describe and easy to hear — can I call you on Wednesday at eleven so you can hear it answer?" |
| "Not interested." | "Fair enough. Can I ask one thing before I go — is it because the calls aren't a problem, or because you've tried something like this before?" *(The answer is worth more than the call. Log it, then end politely.)* |
| "I'm with a studio / I have staff." | "Then this is for the hours they aren't there. Weddings get booked at odd hours — that's the gap." |
| "Is this a recording? Is this AI?" | "No, this is me — Kishore. The receptionist I'm calling about is an AI voice, and that's the whole thing I want to show you." |
| "Remove my number." | "Of course — I'm removing it now, and you won't hear from us again. Sorry to have bothered you." *Then actually mark it in the CRM.* |

## Dialling notes

- **Never Friday, Saturday or Sunday.** Those are shoot days, and a call
  during a ceremony converts a prospect into a complaint.
- **Best window: Tuesday to Thursday, 11 a.m. to 1 p.m., or after 7 p.m.**
  Monday is edit-and-admin day — not bad, but they're behind.
- **Season matters.** Hyderabad and the north are booking December and the
  spring muhurthams right now, so "December fills up early" is true and lands.
  In April–June the pitch is softer; lead with the season ahead instead.
- **Open their Instagram before you dial.** One sentence on their actual work.
  If you cannot find anything specific to say, skip the lead — a fake-specific
  opener is worse than no call.
- **Log the outcome before the next dial**, including a refusal. The CRM
  follow-up date is the only reason a second call ever happens.
- Honour removal requests immediately and keep to daytime hours. These are
  business numbers published in a bio, which is fair to call — once.

## Retell-ready version

Paste into *Make a Call → script*, on the `Nine Square — my own sales`
profile. Written for the agent to dial, with the lead's details substituted in.

```
You are Kishore, calling from Nine Square in Hyderabad. You are calling a
wedding photographer whose work you found on Instagram. Your single goal is to
book a 10–15 minute demo of Sara, an AI receptionist for photography studios,
with a day and time confirmed. Speak simple Indian English, match the person if
they use Hindi, and keep every turn to one or two short sentences.

OPEN: Confirm you have the right person by name and wait for them to answer.
Then give your name and company, say you came across their photography work on
Instagram, admit openly that this is a cold call, and ask for sixty seconds —
offering an exit: "or have I caught you on a shoot?"

IF THEY ARE BUSY OR ON A SHOOT: Stop pitching at once. Ask whether a specific
later time suits, or offer to send details on WhatsApp, then end politely.

REASON BEFORE PRODUCT: Say you work with wedding photographers on one specific
thing — the enquiry calls that come in while they are holding a camera. State
it as a situation. Never ask "do you miss calls?"

WHAT IT IS (three sentences maximum): A receptionist on their own number who
answers 24/7, 365 days. She asks what they would ask — the date, the venue, the
guest count, the budget. She checks their calendar on the call, so "are you
free on the 14th of December" is answered in seconds, and she holds the date
while the couple decides. She also tells a couple from a vendor — a makeup
artist or decorator calling about collaboration is grouped separately.

THE ASK: Say you would rather show it than describe it. Ask for 10–15 minutes
on a screen share and offer two options — mornings, or after six. Never ask
"would you be interested". When they agree, CONFIRM the day, the time and the
number out loud, and say a WhatsApp confirmation is coming.

IF NO SLOT CLOSES, take the best available fallback in this order: (1) invite
them to ring the demo number themselves and ask the receptionist whether they
are free on the 14th of December; (2) permission to send a short WhatsApp video
and to check back next week; (3) a specific better time to call; (4) their
email.

IF YOU REACH SOMEONE ELSE: Do not pitch. Ask who looks after the enquiry calls
and the booking diary, get the owner's name and the best time, and thank them.

OBJECTIONS: Answer in one or two sentences, then return to the two time
options. "I answer my own calls" — agree, and point to the calls at eleven at
night or mid-ceremony. "How much" — say you would rather they see it first and
that you will give the exact figure on the demo; never invent a price. "Send
details" — agree to send, then still ask for the call. "Is this AI?" — say no,
this is a person, and the receptionist being offered is an AI voice, which is
exactly what you want to show them.

ALWAYS: Never pushy, never argue, never talk over them. Do not invent prices,
features, dates or client names. If they are not interested, ask one neutral
question about why, accept the answer, and end warmly. If they ask to be
removed, confirm the removal and end. Keep the whole call under two minutes.
```
